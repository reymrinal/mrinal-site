Chapter I

The Batmobile Chronicles

"Sometimes all you have to do is let your batmobile drive you to places where you are needed."

I am Mrinal Rai. My childhood is the compilation of every 90s kid meme you see on the internet. KV schooling, middle-class family, bunking classes just to lose myself on the PE ground playing football with friends who randomly chose teams and played with pure joy.

While everyone around me chose PCM and marched toward IIT, my batmobile made a different turn. Commerce Maths. Then SRCC — I didn't even know it was a big deal until relatives started congratulating me. The road less travelled syndrome never ruled my head.

"The grass may be greener there — but on my side of the path, the grass is greener too."

Then came the call from Harshit. My best friend since Class 1 — we literally discovered mid-conversation that we'd been friends a year before we thought. He had built Nimble — a tiny ring that transformed 15 feet of space into a human interaction zone. Gestures. Voice. Pure magic.

We won B-Plan competitions. We were high on life. Then reality arrived. Hardware manufacturing in India. No product-market fit. Twenty preschools. Twenty rejections. We had a full-fledged product but didn't know what real problem it countered.

Then Flipkart called. During Big Billion Days, they couldn't track ad-hoc vehicles. Simple problem. We understood it. We had days. We built day and night — CEO, CTO, delivery guy, 24-hour support, all of us wearing every hat.

When that sale ended successfully — I cannot describe that feeling of victory. That's how Intugine was born. Not from a master plan. From a batmobile moment.

Chapter III

The Three Laws of Building

I
Sweet. Simple. Scalable.
The most lovable products aren't the most complex. Clarity over noise. The solution your grandmother understands is usually the right one.
II
Problem First. Always.
Every startup journey begins from a problem statement, not an idea. We learned this the hard way with Nimble — and it became the foundation of everything we built after.
III
How You Do It Matters.
End results depend on a hundred external factors. How you conduct yourself through the journey is the only thing fully in your control. That's why founders build.

Chapter V · Beyond Business

Namaste Democracy

Non-Profit Initiative

"The same principles that build great startups — trust, transparency, participation, accountability — also build great societies."

Namaste Democracy is India's first youth-driven, non-partisan civic initiative — escalating active engagement from classrooms to polling booths to parliament. Because being a builder doesn't end at the product roadmap.

Registered as Jagrati Foundation, it addresses the most overlooked step in democracy: political literacy. Based in Bangalore and Bhopal, the movement has already reached 1.8M+ people.
